Scrum can make a team faster at building the wrong product. Roman Pichler focuses on the role that prevents that: the product owner.

The book is short and practical. Its argument is clear. Product ownership is not backlog administration. It is deciding what deserves to be built, why it matters, and what should wait.

<!-- more -->

### The Product Owner Owns Value

- **One person owns the product decisions.** The team contributes knowledge, users bring problems, stakeholders bring constraints. The product owner turns those inputs into one direction.
- **Ownership needs authority.** A product owner who cannot say no becomes a messenger between stakeholders and developers. The role only works when decisions can be made and defended.
- **The goal is outcomes.** Shipping more backlog items means little if customers do not benefit. Progress is the value created, not the amount of work completed.

### Start With a Product Vision

- **The vision explains the destination.** Before discussing features, the team needs to know who the product serves, which problem it solves, and why it should exist.
- **Keep it concrete.** A useful vision guides trade-offs. When two ideas compete, the team can ask which one moves the product closer to that destination.
- **Share it often.** The vision is not a document written once. It gives every [sprint](/readings/zombie-scrum-survival-guide/) a reason.

### The Backlog Is a Living Plan

- **Order matters more than volume.** A long backlog creates the illusion of certainty. The product owner keeps the most valuable and risky work near the top, then lets weak ideas fall away.
- **Detail arrives late.** Near-term items need enough clarity for the team to act. Distant items should stay rough because learning will change them.
- **Refinement is collaborative.** Developers expose technical risk. Stakeholders explain business needs. Customers reveal whether the problem is real. The product owner keeps the final call.

### Releases Create Learning

- **A release is not a deadline.** It is a chance to put a coherent product increment in front of customers and learn from real use.
- **Plan around goals.** Release planning connects several sprints to one customer outcome. [Scrum](/readings/scrum-the-art-of-doing-twice/) supplies the rhythm, not the purpose.
- **Adjust when evidence changes.** A plan is useful until reality teaches the team something better. Product ownership means changing direction without losing the vision.

### Collaboration Is the Job

- **Stay close to the team.** Product decisions cannot be delegated through tickets. Questions appear during the work, and delayed answers become assumptions.
- **Bring stakeholders together.** Competing requests do not disappear inside a backlog. The product owner makes the trade-offs visible and explains what will not be done.
- **Invite feedback early.** [Show work while it is still cheap to change](/blog/ship-show-ask/). A sprint review is useful when it changes the next decision, not when it performs progress.

The product owner does not protect a backlog. The product owner protects the product.
