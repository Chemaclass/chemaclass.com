# Slop: what to cut before delivering prose

Adapted from [stop-slop](https://github.com/hardikpandya/stop-slop) by Hardik Pandya (MIT License, Copyright (c) 2025 Hardik Pandya). Applies to English and Spanish.

## Phrases to cut

Delete the phrase and state the point.

| Kind | English | Spanish |
|---|---|---|
| Throat-clearing | "Here's the thing", "Here's what/why...", "The truth is", "It turns out", "Let me be clear", "The real X is", "Can we talk about" | "La cosa es", "Lo cierto es que", "Resulta que", "Seamos claros", "La verdadera X es", "Hablemos de" |
| Emphasis crutch | "Full stop.", "Let that sink in.", "Make no mistake", "This matters because", "Here's why that matters" | "Punto.", "Piénsalo.", "No te equivoques", "Esto importa porque" |
| Filler frame | "At its core", "In today's X", "It's worth noting", "At the end of the day", "When it comes to", "In a world where", "The reality is" | "En el fondo", "En el mundo actual", "Cabe destacar", "Al final del día", "A fin de cuentas", "Cuando se trata de", "La realidad es que" |
| Meta-commentary | "Hint:", "Spoiler:", "Plot twist:", "Let me walk you through", "In this section we'll", "As we'll see", "The rest of this post explains", "But that's another post", "X is a feature, not a bug" | "Pista:", "Spoiler:", "Te lo explico paso a paso", "En esta sección veremos", "Como veremos", "Pero eso es otro post" |
| Fake sincerity | "I promise", "honestly", "to be honest", "creeps in" | "Te lo prometo", "sinceramente", "para ser honesto" |
| Telling, not showing | "This is genuinely hard", "This is what X actually looks like", "actually matters" | "Esto es difícil de verdad", "Así es como se ve X de verdad" |
| Vague declarative | "The reasons are structural", "The implications are significant", "The stakes are high" | "Las razones son estructurales", "Las implicaciones son enormes" |
| Business jargon | navigate, unpack, lean into, landscape, game-changer, double down, deep dive, take a step back, moving forward, circle back | navegar (retos), desgranar, apostar fuerte, el panorama, un antes y un después, a futuro |

Adverbs that only add emphasis or softness: really, just, literally, genuinely, honestly, simply, actually, deeply, truly, fundamentally, inherently, inevitably, interestingly, importantly, crucially. Spanish: realmente, simplemente, básicamente, literalmente, sinceramente, verdaderamente, fundamentalmente, claramente, sin duda.

A vague declarative is fixed by naming the thing: "The implications are significant" becomes "Customers lose a day of bookings".

## Structures to break

| Pattern | Example | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Binary contrast | "Not because X. Because Y." / "The answer isn't X. It's Y." / "X isn't the problem. Y is." / "not just X but Y" / "stops being X and starts being Y" | State Y. |
| Negative listing | "It wasn't X. It wasn't Y. It was Z." | State Z. |
| Dramatic fragments | "Speed. Quality. Cost." / "That's it. That's the trick." / "X. And Y. And Z." | One complete sentence. |
| Rhetorical setup | "What if...?", "Think about it:", "Here's what I mean:", "And that's okay." | Make the point. |
| Question answered at once | "The fix? Delete it." | "Delete it." |
| False agency | "the decision emerges", "the culture shifts", "the data tells us", "the complaint becomes a fix" | Name who decided, changed, read or fixed it. |
| Narrator from a distance | "Nobody designed this.", "People tend to...", "This is why..." | Put the reader in it: "You don't sit down and decide to...". |
| Passive voice | "Mistakes were made", "It is believed that" | Name the actor and put them first. |
| Wh- or "So" opener | "What makes this hard is...", "So, ..." | Lead with the subject: "The constraint is...". |
| Triads | "faster, cheaper, better" | Keep the one or two that are true. |
| Punchline endings | Every paragraph ends on a quotable one-liner | End most paragraphs on their last fact. |
| Quotable lines | "Skills capture what to do. Rules capture what not to do." | If it reads like a pull-quote, rewrite it as a plain sentence. |
| Lazy extremes | always, never, everyone, nobody | Give the specific case or count. |

## Quick checks

Run these on the final draft:

1. Any emphasis or softening adverb? Cut it.
2. Any passive verb, or a thing doing a human verb? Name the person.
3. Any "not X, it's Y" or "X isn't Y" contrast? State Y.
4. Any throat-clearing or meta-commentary from the table? Delete it.
5. Any stacked fragments or three same-length sentences in a row? Merge or vary them.
6. Do most paragraphs end on a punchline? Rewrite half of them to end on a fact.
7. Any line written to be quoted? Rewrite it plain.
8. Any vague claim ("this matters", "the stakes are high")? Name the specific thing or cut it.
9. Any em or en dash? Remove it.

Score the draft 1 to 10 on directness, rhythm, trust in the reader, sounding human, and density. Below 35 of 50, revise.

## Before and after

Before: "Here's the thing: building products is hard. Not because the technology is complex. Because people are complex. Let that sink in."

After: "Building the product took us a month. Agreeing on what to build took three."

Before: "What if the best teams don't optimize for productivity? Here's what I mean: they optimize for learning. Think about it."

After: "The best teams I worked with optimized for learning: they paired on every new service."
